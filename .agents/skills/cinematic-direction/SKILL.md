# Cinematic direction and editing — Preview integration

Apply this skill at initial Plan, after Pose/Lip in Scene Review and Camera Replan, and at Cam Check. Follow the current Preview rules card and validate against actual scene evidence.

## 1. Story beats, not a timer
- Read timed narration/dialogue in context. Identify what the audience needs to understand, whose emotion matters, what action/location/prop the line describes, and what changes at each beat.
- Make an initial coverage intent, not final coordinates. After staging and motion, inspect fresh real scene views and blocking at representative times; compare actual action, props, faces, background and occlusion. Replan camera positions, targets, focal length, shot sizes, and cut times from that evidence.
- If the narration introduces a hair salon, establish its real frontage or interior, then show the actual work (hands, hair, mirror, tools, customer/stylist relationship). Use a close-up only if an emotion or detail merits it. Never show an unrelated prop, fake action or rear facade as a substitute.

## 2. Camera vocabulary and restraint
- Character lens range 75–150 mm; use contextual/action coverage approximately 50–85 mm and location coverage approximately 24–50 mm when justified. Final framing, perspective and available room determine lens and distance; numbers are guidance, not a rotation schedule. Respect existing no-unmotivated-full-body and no-awkward-side-by-side rules.
- Vary actual subject scale and framing: contextual chest-up, medium close, close, OTS, meaningful insert/establishing. Same-size reverse shots of different speakers are valid in a motivated exchange; a whole sequence of equal-size faces fails. Don't insert a cut merely to satisfy diversity.
- Keep camera motion subtle, one direction, no whip or bouncing. Hold a good composition if the meaning is unchanged; maintain eyeline and screen direction.

## 3. Editorial pacing
- Normal shot duration roughly 6–12 seconds, longer 12–20 seconds when one meaningful beat continues; short 3–5 second reaction/detail only when justified. Do not cut every 5 seconds mechanically. No 1–2 second staccato sequences for ordinary narration. Keep existing minimum duration and end hold until revised validator is tested.
- Prefer a meaningful phrase boundary or change of action/emotion. Let the previous shot breathe; a camera movement is not a reason to cut. Preserve complete audio and speaker coverage. Across a part boundary avoid duplicated image or abrupt changes.
- Each proposed cut must state why the next image improves comprehension/emotion, and why holding the current shot is insufficient. If no good reason, merge or hold.

## 4. Two-pass QA
- Pass A: story fidelity and visual coverage — actual subjects, actions, setting, props, dialogue and continuity; no fabricated movement or incorrect detail.
- Pass B: watch in order as an editor, not a checklist of isolated attractive frames. Inspect actual start/middle/end stills and a moving preview or existing render where available. Check shot size rhythm, pace, occlusion, motion, face, light, cut continuity and audio. If the cut is dizzying, repetitive or narratively unclear, repair and recheck. Never claim playback review from stills alone.
- Sleep Mode must still pass both QA reviews under the user's explicit scoped authorization; normal user approval remains unchanged. No fake QA or approval, no automatic activation.


When Scene Review identifies missing narrative props, consult blender-prop-production and the installed Blender skills; create and QA the asset before camera replan. Same quality gate in both modes.
